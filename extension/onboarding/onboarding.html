Before FocusGuard AI starts

FocusGuard AI notices when you drift from the task you chose and gives you a gentle nudge back. To do that it needs to look at your tabs. Here is exactly what it does. Nothing is observed until you click Start FocusGuard AI.

What FocusGuard AI looks at, in this browser

  • The site name and title of the tab you're on, and when you switch tabs.
  • Whether you're at the computer, so it never nudges you while you're away.
  • It skips banking, health, password-manager and government sites, incognito windows and Chrome's own pages. You can add more sites to skip in Settings.

What never leaves your browser

Page titles, full web addresses, the tabs to reopen after a break, your break notes, any AI key, and the moment-by-moment record of what you did.

What is sent to FocusGuard AI's server, only after you sign in

  • Your goals.
  • Your context names, their kind (work, learning, personal) and state, and the names of sites in each context, like github.com. Never full addresses.
  • Your settings and daily totals, like focus minutes, nudges and breaks.

Sent over HTTPS. Not sold, not used for ads. You can export or delete everything from the dashboard. Read the privacy policy.

Optional AI help

AI can recognise sites FocusGuard AI's list doesn't know and suggest context names. It's off unless you turn it on.

AI help

You can also connect a local model or your own API key later in Settings. Then the site name, cleaned-up page title, context name and today's goal titles go only to the address you choose.

Nudges on distracting sites

To show a small card in the corner of distracting sites (like social media and video sites), Chrome needs your permission for those sites only. Without it, FocusGuard AI uses Chrome notifications instead.
